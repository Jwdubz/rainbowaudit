What your site is costing you — Rainbow Gardens

I'm Jarrett, a web designer here in Las Vegas. I went through your site the way a couple planning their wedding would, on a phone. Here's what I saw.

1. On a phone, the homepage takes a very long time to show up. When I tested it on a simulated phone connection, the screen stayed mostly empty for a long stretch before your photos appeared. Couples comparing venues on their phones usually don't wait. They hit back and tap the next venue.

2. The only next step is "Contact Us." Someone who's ready can't see a price range, check whether their date is open, or book a walk-through. Every interested couple has to fill out a form and wait. Some will. Many will book a venue that let them do more right away.

3. A placeholder page is live. There's a blog post with a "lorem ipsum" web address and a generic title, and the newest post I could find is from 2019. It makes the business look quieter than it is.

4. Your beautiful room photos are invisible to Google. Most of the venue photos have no description, so Google and people using screen readers can't tell what they are. A locked "Forms" page also shows up to Google.

5. Your FAQ sends people to other businesses. It lists other vendors' phone numbers and emails, which can pull couples away from you.

What's working: your phone number is tappable on every page, the quote form asks the right questions, and the pages are set up so Google can read them.

Unmeasured: how many people visit, where you rank, and whether quote requests actually reach your inbox. I didn't submit your form or look at your private data.

What I checked on your site

  • Found
    Not blocking AI crawlers

    Your robots.txt has an empty Disallow rule, which allows everything. I tested 11 well-known AI and search crawler names (GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, CCBot, Bytespider, Amazonbot) and none is blocked.

  • Found
    Name, address and phone are plain text

    4125 W. Charleston Blvd., Las Vegas, NV 89102 and (702) 878-4646 sit at the top of the homepage as readable text, and the phone number is tappable. It is the only number on the page.

  • Gap
    Structured data doesn't describe the business

    The pages carry only generic website and page markup. Nothing says you are a wedding venue, and your phone number, address, hours and social profiles aren't in that data.

  • Gap
    No Google listing link on the homepage

    I found no link or embedded map pointing to your Google Maps listing on the homepage. That link is one of the ways search tools connect a website to a business.

  • Gap
    No llms.txt file

    rainbowgardenslv.com/llms.txt returns "not found". It is a newer, optional file that some AI tools read to understand a site, so this is a small gap.

  • Note
    Homepage text is short and the title is generic

    The homepage has about 230 words of readable text. The page title is "Rainbow Gardens - Banquet Facility" and the main heading is "Welcome to Rainbow Gardens". Neither says wedding venue or Las Vegas, though the text lower on the page does.

These are plain checks anyone can repeat on October 15, 2026: what the site publishes, not a score.

What I couldn't measure

  • Rankings in Google or Google Maps.
  • What ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity or Google's AI answers say when asked directly. I didn't have access to ask them, so the searches above are a stand-in.
  • How many people search for these terms or how many visit your site.
  • Your Google listing itself: its reviews, photos, hours and phone number as Google shows them.

What I'd fix first

Add local-business structured data (wedding venue, with your address, phone and hours), link your Google listing from the homepage, and rewrite the title and main heading to say wedding venue in Las Vegas. That gives search tools clean facts to quote. I can't promise where it will place you.
